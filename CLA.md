### How acceptance is recorded, and why it is a signature

Acceptance is given by a comment in a pull request, from the contributor's own
GitHub account, in the words set out in `CONTRIBUTING.md` (making the comment is
not the only way a licence can arise — see the preamble — but it is the way the
record is made, and the form section 101A asks for). That comment is a
signature for the purposes of section 101A of the Copyright, Designs and Patents
Act 1988:

* it is **in writing** — section 178 of the Copyright, Designs and Patents Act
  1988, in the same Part as section 101A, defines "writing" as including any form
  of notation or code, whether by hand or otherwise and regardless of the method
  or medium of recording, so a typed comment is writing; the Interpretation Act
  1978 (Schedule 1) reaches the same result by including words represented "in a
  visible form";
* it is **signed** — neither Act defines "sign", so the common law applies: it
  asks only that the mark be made with the intention of authenticating the
  document. The acceptance phrase is a fixed, prepared text, deliberately
  submitted to accept a named version of this Agreement, not an incidental
  remark; the same reasoning is the one the Law Commission followed in its 2019
  report on the electronic execution of documents, and the Court of Appeal in
  *Golden Ocean Group Ltd v Salgaocar Mining Industries Pvt Ltd* [2012] EWCA Civ
  265;
* it is **authenticated** — the comment comes from a GitHub account whose numeric
  id cannot be changed or reused, a stronger link to a person than a typed name,
  which anyone can write. The "on behalf of" limb of section 101A(1)(b)(i)
  matters only where one person signs for another (an employer for its
  employee, section 7.2 above); here the signatory signs for itself;
* it is **admissible** — section 7 of the Electronic Communications Act 2000
  makes an electronic signature admissible in evidence on the authenticity and
  integrity of the communication;
* it is **fixed to an exact text** — the version is pinned by the tag on the
  document and, because a tag can be moved, the record also takes the commit the
  accepted text stood at, so it shows which words were accepted.

The same comment also carries the consent and waiver relied on under section 87
of that Act: section 87(2) requires a waiver of moral rights to be in writing
and signed, and the analysis above applies to it in the same way.

The record kept for each acceptance therefore holds the account login and
numeric id, the time, the version and commit, and the pull request, comment and
repository; see the header of this Agreement. What the contributor is told about
this record, and what they can ask for, is set out in `CONTRIBUTING.md`.

**No name is typed in the comment.** A typed name proves nothing (anyone can
write any name) and is not needed, because the authenticated account is the
identifier. The exact wording of the acceptance phrase is a mechanism of the
bot, not a term of this Agreement; it may be changed without a new version of
this Agreement.
